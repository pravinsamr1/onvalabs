At a certain size the question stops being which tool to add and becomes how many systems you can keep reconciling. Finance closes the month from exports, operations tracks stock in its own spreadsheet, HR holds the only accurate list of who works where, and the numbers in a board pack depend on which system they came from. Integrations help, but they do not fix the fact that there is no single record.

An ERP puts those functions on one platform with one data model, so a purchase order, the stock it creates and the invoice it generates are the same record seen from three angles. We deliver ours as SaaS: subscribed per user, hosted in Sydney, updated centrally, and configured to your structure rather than rebuilt from scratch for each client.

What's included

  • Modular scopeFinance, purchasing, inventory, operations, projects and people — take the modules you need and add others later.
  • One data modelCustomers, suppliers, items and staff exist once, so reporting reconciles without a spreadsheet in the middle.
  • Configuration, not reinventionYour chart of accounts, approval limits, sites, cost centres and workflows set up to match how you are structured.
  • Migration and cutover planningOpening balances, master data and historical records moved across, with a parallel period before you rely on it.
  • Training and adoption supportRole-based training and floor support during go-live, because an ERP nobody uses properly is the expensive kind.
  • Australian hosting and privacyRun from Sydney data centres, with access control and retention designed around the Privacy Act 1988 and the Australian Privacy Principles.

This tier suits you if

  • Finance, operations and people data live in separate systems that disagree.
  • Month-end depends on exports and manual reconciliation.
  • You operate across multiple sites, entities or cost centres.
  • You would rather subscribe per user than fund a capital build.

Frequently asked questions

How is SaaS ERP different from a custom-built system?

A custom system is written for you and you own the code. SaaS ERP is a platform you subscribe to, configured to your organisation and updated centrally for everyone. SaaS gets you live faster and spreads the cost; custom gives you total control. We will walk you through which trade-off fits.

How long does an ERP implementation take?

Longer than software people expect, because most of the work is data, decisions and adoption rather than code. Timeframes depend on how many modules you take, how clean your existing data is and how quickly decisions get made. We stage it by module so value arrives before the whole thing is finished.

Where does our data live?

In Australian data centres. That keeps the platform quick for local users and your data onshore, which matters for organisations with data sovereignty obligations. Access control, retention and breach response are designed around the Privacy Act 1988 and the Notifiable Data Breaches scheme.
