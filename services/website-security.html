What we do

Website security services

Six areas of ordinary maintenance that account for most real-world website compromises, none of them dramatic, and all of them easier to do than to explain afterwards.

SSL certificates and encryption

SSL certificate setup and renewal, with HTTPS enforced everywhere, modern TLS settings, and no mixed content quietly loading over an unencrypted connection behind the padlock.

Website hardening and access control

Unused accounts removed, admin paths restricted, multi-factor authentication required, and each person's permissions cut back to what their job actually needs and nothing more.

Patching on a schedule

Core software, plugins, themes and server packages updated on an agreed schedule and tested on a copy first, because out-of-date components cause more compromises than clever attacks.

Web application firewall

A web application firewall filters common attack traffic before it reaches your site, and rate limiting slows the automated login attempts that run against every site constantly.

Malware scanning and removal

Scheduled scanning, plus malware removal when something does get in: the files cleaned, the way in found and closed, and the site resubmitted for review.

Backups and incident response

Offsite backups, restores tested rather than assumed, and a written plan naming who is called, what gets checked and in what order when something goes wrong.

Common questions

Can you guarantee our website will not be hacked?

No. Nobody can, and we would rather say so plainly than sell you a promise we cannot keep. Software has flaws, people reuse passwords, and attackers automate. What security work does is close the openings that are actually exploited, make an intrusion harder and slower, and make sure you notice quickly.

What are our obligations if customer data is exposed?

Under the Privacy Act 1988 and the Notifiable Data Breaches scheme, an eligible data breach involving personal information likely to cause serious harm must be assessed and notified. We are not lawyers and do not give legal advice. We build the logging and response steps that let you establish what happened and when.

Is an SSL certificate enough on its own?

No. An SSL certificate encrypts traffic between the browser and the server, which matters, but it does nothing about an out-of-date plugin, a weak admin password, or a form that accepts anything typed into it. Encryption is one layer. Patching, access control, firewalling, scanning and tested backups are the rest.
