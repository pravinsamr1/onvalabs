What we build

Portal types and features

Six kinds of portal work. Customer portal development rarely arrives alone: most organisations start with one audience and add another once people stop ringing to ask for things.

Customer and client portals

Orders, quotes, invoices and job history in one logged-in place. Client portal software like this lets a customer check at nine at night instead of ringing on Monday.

Member and association portals

Member portal development for associations and clubs: renewals, membership status, event bookings, committee documents and a directory members update themselves rather than emailing the secretary.

Supplier and partner portals

Purchase orders, delivery confirmations, compliance certificates and agreed pricing shared with suppliers and resellers, replacing the spreadsheet somebody emails around on Friday afternoons.

Staff and intranet portals

Rosters, policies, leave requests, onboarding checklists and site documents for staff, including the ones who work from a ute rather than from a desk.

Secure document portal

A secure document portal for contracts, statements and signed forms, with upload, version history and an access log showing who opened what and when.

Self service and dashboards

A self service portal where people update their own details, lodge requests and track progress, with dashboards showing only the numbers that audience is entitled to see.

Common questions

How is a portal different from a website?

A website is public and the same for everyone. A portal sits behind a login and shows each person only their own information: their invoices, their documents, their job status. That difference changes how the whole thing is built, because now you are deciding who may see what, and proving it on every request.

How do you control who sees what?

Role-based access control. Every person belongs to a role, each role is granted specific permissions, and the portal checks permission on every request rather than merely hiding a menu item. Roles are mapped and signed off during design. Personal information is handled under the Privacy Act 1988 and the Australian Privacy Principles.

Can the portal use data from our existing systems?

Usually. If your accounting, job management or inventory system offers an API, the portal reads live data and nobody retypes anything. Where a platform has no API, we look at scheduled exports or a middleware layer instead. We check what is actually available before promising anyone a live connection.
