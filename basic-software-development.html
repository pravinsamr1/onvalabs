Three tiers of software development

  1. Basic Software Development
  2. Custom Business Software
  3. ERP Systems — SaaS

Somewhere in your business there is a spreadsheet doing a job it was never meant to do. Someone rekeys the same figures every Monday, a paper form gets carried between two buildings, and the one person who understands the macro is on leave. It works, mostly, until it doesn't — and the cost is invisible because it is spread across everybody's week.

Basic software development is the smallest useful fix. We build a single-purpose tool that does one job well: a form that captures clean data, a calculator that applies your rules consistently, a small internal app that replaces the shared spreadsheet. Short scope, short build, and no obligation to buy a system you do not need yet.

What's included

  • A scoped single purposeWe agree in writing what the tool does and, just as importantly, what it does not do.
  • Clean data captureWeb or mobile forms with validation, so what goes in is usable rather than something to clean up later.
  • Your business rules, applied consistentlyPricing, eligibility, scoring or scheduling logic encoded once instead of remembered differently by each person.
  • Simple reporting and exportThe views your team actually looks at, plus CSV or Excel export for everything else.
  • Secure accessLogins, roles and an activity trail sized to the tool rather than bolted on.
  • Handover and documentationSource code, deployment notes and a short admin guide, all in your name.

This tier suits you if

  • The problem is one process, not the whole business.
  • A handful of people use it, not every department.
  • You want something working in weeks, not a programme of work.
  • You would rather prove the idea cheaply before committing further.

Frequently asked questions

How is this different from Custom Business Software?

Scale and scope. Basic development builds one tool for one job with a small user group. Custom Business Software is a multi-user system with roles, workflow across teams and integrations into your other software. If more than one department depends on it, you are in the next tier.

Can it grow later?

Yes, and that is the usual path. We build on standard frameworks and databases rather than throwaway scripts, so a tool that proves its worth can be extended into a full system without starting again. Nothing about this tier locks you out of the next one.

What if a product already exists that does this?

Then buy it, and we will say so. Custom software earns its place when your process is genuinely unusual or the off-the-shelf options force you to change how you work. We would rather point you at a good product than build a worse copy of it.
