Why it exists

You cannot manage a distributed team you cannot see

A field IT team is a genuinely hard operational problem. Everyone is busy, everyone is somewhere else, and the only honest answer to “are we covering our sites?” is usually a shrug and a promise to find out.

✗Coverage by group chat

  • The only record of who went where is a message thread that scrolls away by Thursday.
  • Nobody can tell which sites have not been visited this month until someone complains.
  • Remote work is invisible, so the technicians who fix the most look like they do the least.
  • Monthly reporting is a manual reconstruction that takes half a day and is wrong anyway.
  • Public holidays differ by state, and the roster does not know that.

✓Coverage on a live board

  • One screen shows active now, technicians reporting, on-site versus remote, sites covered and completed today.
  • Coverage percentage by state and by site, for any period, in one click.
  • Remote work is a first-class mode, logged and counted exactly like an on-site visit.
  • Reports are generated, not reconstructed, and export as CSV or PDF.
  • State-based public holidays are held in the system — including the ones only one state observes.
Inside the platform

Five screens that replace the status meeting

Built for the person who has to answer for coverage, and for the technician who just wants to log the day and move on.

01

The live board

Real-time coverage across every site — who is where right now, on site or remote, and what they are working on. Active now, technicians reporting, on-site and remote counts, sites covered today and completed jobs across the top. Per-technician cards show the current site, mode, live status and work categories, with regional coverage bars and a live activity feed beside them.

Twenty seconds to log a day

The technician view. Choose the clinic, pick on-site or remote, set the live status, tick the work categories that apply and add a line of description. Several clinics can be logged for the same day. If logging a visit takes longer than a coffee order, nobody does it — so this screen is deliberately the least impressive one in the product.
