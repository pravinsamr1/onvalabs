On a legal site

Where a legal website has to be careful

Six parts of a solicitor website that decide whether someone with a real problem picks up the phone, written for firms that would rather be accurate than loud.

Practice-area pages that explain

One page per area of law, in plain English: what the matter usually involves, what happens first, and what the firm does about it. Not a list of headings nobody reads.

Lawyer profiles with credentials

Admission details, areas of practice, courts appeared in, languages spoken and professional memberships. Enough for a prospective client to picture who would hold the file.

Confidential enquiry handling

Forms that ask only for what is needed to open a conversation, with clear wording about what happens to the information and who inside the firm receives it.

Secure document exchange

A client area for sending and receiving documents, so sensitive material is not sitting as an attachment on somebody's phone and in three separate inboxes.

Accessible by design, not afterwards

Contrast, type size, keyboard navigation and screen-reader structure tested against WCAG 2.2 AA, because legal information is read by people in difficulty on whatever screen is nearest.

Content that demonstrates expertise

Articles and guides on the questions clients actually ask, written or reviewed by your solicitors, with structured data so search engines can tell who stands behind them.

Common questions

Can we publish client testimonials and case results?

Legal advertising is regulated by the legal professional body in each state and territory, and obligations exist around what a firm may say about outcomes. Those obligations are your firm's to confirm; Onva Labs is not a law firm and does not advise on them. Practically, we build pages that establish credibility through credentials, depth and clear writing instead.

Is an enquiry form on the website confidential?

That depends entirely on how it is built, which is why we treat it as a design decision rather than a default. We encrypt data in transit, limit what the form asks for, control who inside the firm receives it, and avoid sending enquiry content as plain email. The Privacy Act 1988 and the Australian Privacy Principles apply throughout.

How do clients send us documents without using email?

Through a secure client area rather than attachments. The client signs in, uploads the file, and your team collects it from one place with a record of what arrived and when. It keeps documents out of multiple inboxes and off personal phones, and it gives both sides somewhere to look when a file goes missing.
