Incident response fails in the paperwork, not the panic

Most teams handle the technical response well. What breaks is everything that has to be true afterwards — who approved containment, what evidence was collected, which control was actually improved, and whether the residual risk was ever accepted by someone senior enough to accept it.

✗Life on spreadsheets and inboxes

  • The incident log lives in one workbook, the evidence in a shared drive, and the approvals in an email thread nobody can find.
  • Lessons learned get written once, filed, and never turn into a control change.
  • Nobody can answer “how much risk did we actually retire?” with a number.
  • The audit trail is whatever people remember, reconstructed under time pressure.
  • Reporting clocks start ticking while the team is still deciding who owns the record.

✓Life on Onva Incident Command

  • One record per incident carrying timeline, evidence, root cause, actions, approvals and sign-off together.
  • Lessons learned are structured fields that open control improvements and risk register updates automatically.
  • Inherent versus residual risk is calculated, aggregated and shown as a percentage the board understands.
  • Every view, search, export and field change is written to an append-only log with its session.
  • Ownership and the approval chain are set the moment the incident is declared.
