What we build

Six things that matter more than the homepage photo

The parts of an accounting website that decide whether an enquiry arrives ready to work with, or arrives as three emails asking what you charge.

Service pages by client type

Individuals, sole traders, SMSF trustees and companies want different answers. Separate pages let each group read work that matches them instead of one undifferentiated services list.

Secure document upload, not email

Clients send payslips, statements and identity documents. We set up upload that keeps those files out of mailboxes and tells your team when something new has landed.

A client portal people will use

A logged-in area showing what you still need, what has been lodged and what is waiting on the client, so fewer reminders are chased by phone each week.

Booking that survives July

Your calendar connected to the site, with appointment types, durations and intake questions set so tax-time bookings arrive ready rather than needing a follow-up email first.

Fee information written plainly

Fixed fees, ranges, or a short explanation of how you quote and what moves the number. Enquiries then arrive from people who already understand the shape.

Tax-time content prepared early

Deduction explainers, checklists and deadline pages written in autumn and indexed well before the June rush, so they are already earning visits when searching begins.

Common questions

Should we publish our fees on the website?

Many firms do, and it filters enquiries well. You can publish fixed fees, ranges, or simply explain how you quote and what changes the number. Any of those beats silence, because people shopping for an accountant are comparing. Advertising a financial service carries regulatory obligations your firm should confirm for itself; we build whatever wording you settle on.

How do clients send us documents without emailing them?

We add upload to your site or client portal, so files go into a controlled location instead of sitting in mailboxes. You can request specific documents, see what has arrived, and chase what has not. The Privacy Act 1988 and the Australian Privacy Principles shape how that information is collected, transmitted and stored, and we document those choices.

When should we start getting ready for tax time?

Earlier than most firms do. Pages written in late June compete from a standing start. Explainers, checklists and deadline content published in autumn have time to be indexed and found before the July surge. We also check booking capacity and page speed beforehand, because a slow site during your busiest fortnight costs real appointments.
