Why it exists

Most LMS platforms have two roles and a lot of workarounds

Teacher and student covers about a third of the people who need access. The rest — teaching assistants, graders, course builders, department heads, parents, observers, support staff, external moderators — end up sharing logins or getting far more access than anyone intended.

✗The two-role problem

  • A teaching assistant gets full instructor access because there is no other option.
  • Parents and observers either see everything or nothing at all.
  • Course builders need an administrator to publish, so publishing becomes a bottleneck.
  • Department-level reporting means exporting everything and pivoting it by hand.
  • Security settings live in a configuration file nobody has opened since implementation.

✓Thirteen roles, properly scoped

  • System, institution and department administrators each get a dashboard scoped to their level.
  • Instructor, teaching assistant, course builder and grader are separate profiles with separate permissions.
  • Parents and observers get a read view of exactly the learner they are entitled to see.
  • Analytics roll up by course, department and institution without an export.
  • Security posture is a panel on the dashboard — hashing, lockout, timeout, MFA, self-registration, audit.

Questions

Straight answers

Because that is how many distinct relationships people actually have with a course. Teaching assistant, grader and course builder do genuinely different jobs from an instructor, and parents and observers need a bounded read view. Collapsing them forces over-permissioning, which is the most common security finding in an LMS review.

It carries the ERP layer — organisations, hierarchy, terms, enrolments, categories, competencies and reporting — so many institutions run it as the system of record. Where a dedicated SIS already exists, the documented API and OAuth integration keeps rosters, enrolments and results synchronised in both directions.

It is an institution-controlled space for AI-assisted content authoring and feedback, with the governance settings — what is enabled, for whom, and on what content — held by the institution administrator rather than by individual instructors.

As a distinct role profile, entitled to one learner and scoped to a read view of that learner’s progress, attendance and results. Access is granted deliberately, bounded by the entitlement, and recorded in the audit log like any other access.

Accessibility is a first-class administration area within the platform rather than a compliance checkbox, covering the learner, instructor and administrator experiences. We will walk your team through the current conformance position against your specific obligations during evaluation.
