The workbook worked — until it didn’t

A spreadsheet register is genuinely fine at twenty risks and one owner. It falls apart at the moment you need cross-sheet references to hold, control effectiveness to recalculate, evidence to be attached, and three different people to update it in the same week.

✗The workbook at scale

  • Cross-sheet formulas break silently, and nobody notices until the audit.
  • Residual risk is whatever was last typed in, not what the controls actually produce.
  • Mitigation plans sit on a second tab with no live link back to the risk they treat.
  • Evidence is a folder path in a comment, if it is anywhere at all.
  • There is one version, one editor, and a very nervous risk manager.

✓The register as a live system

  • Residual risk and control effectiveness are calculated from the model every time the inputs move.
  • Mitigation plans map to a risk, and the function and risk-to-mitigate fields auto-fetch from that link.
  • Evidence is an object in the system, counted on the dashboard and attached to the treatment.
  • Every risk has a named owner, a treatment decision and a next review date the platform tracks.
  • Revision history under document control shows what changed, when and by whom.
